Only these two Roman roles use the licensed source pack so far. Other factions retain their procedural prototypes. Rams and catapults are inspection models; Academy and Workshop progression remain planned.

Rebuild the prototypes

Install the project npm dependencies, Blender 4.5 with COLLADA import, and Python 3 with Pillow. Run these from the repository root. Replace blender and python with their installed executable paths if needed.

node scripts/fetch-unit-references.mjs
python scripts/convert-reference-textures.py
blender --background --python scripts/blender/cache_reference_imports.py
blender --background --python scripts/blender/build_reference_prototypes.py -- --edition reference-v3
# PowerShell: use the same unused edition for optimization
$env:PERIS_REFERENCE_EDITION='reference-v3'
node scripts/prepare-reference-prototypes.mjs

The builder preserves existing sources. Choose another unused edition if reference-v3 already exists. Export and optimization write separate outputs.

03 · From source to battle

A practical production sequence

Complete the two prototype units, inspect their exported animations, and measure them in the battle before reproducing the process across factions.

  1. Approve the silhouette

    Use a licensed base with coherent anatomy. Build Peris' identity through proportions, armor, cloth, weapons, and faction motifs. Review the infantry and cavalry from the game camera before expanding the roster.

    Peris art direction proposal · informed by the references above

  2. Build a modular rig

    Share compatible skeletons within a body family. Fit equipment to named attachments. Check joint deformation in action poses.

    Sources: 0 A.D. actor · NVIDIA crowd rendering

  3. Fit the complete cavalry unit

    Seat the pelvis on the saddle, fit thighs around the horse, and place boots and hands against their equipment. Use a rider attachment and shared animation clock. Review idle, locomotion, and attack from front and side views.

    Sources: 0 A.D. rider attachment · Three.js clip synchronization

  4. Author the game materials

    Use base color, normal detail, and metallic/roughness response. Distinguish metal, skin, leather, and cloth under neutral lighting. Convert the downloaded legacy specular materials deliberately; specular images need interpretation.

    Source: Khronos glTF materials · conversion approach is a Peris proposal

  5. Export and compare the GLB

    Preserve the editable Blender source. Export the intended unit collection with its skin and named clips. Inspect, optimize to a separate file, validate, and compare the export in Three.js with the accepted source.

    Sources: Three.js GLB workflow · Animation clips

  6. Measure the army renderer

    Share compatible assets, cull offscreen soldiers, and reduce distant detail. After validating individual clips, consider bone animation textures with per-instance timing. Measure armies on named hardware.

    Source: NVIDIA GPU Gems 3, Chapter 2. Its DirectX 10 method needs adaptation and measurement in Three.js/WebGL.

04 · Review criteria

What makes the prototypes ready?

  • Believable anatomy and equipment from the battle camera.
  • Stable rider contact in idle, movement, and attack clips.
  • Complete clips, textures, attachments, and bind pose after export.
  • Visual comparison of source, optimized model, and detail levels.
  • Recorded hardware, viewport, unit count, quality, FPS, draw calls, and triangles.
  • Independent verification of existing formation commands, combat, and saves.

# The Wild Crown — native world-map sample

Open the existing Vite game at `/?map-preview=1`, or choose **Explore the world map sample** on the launcher. This is an isolated, accelerated sample for evaluating the new map direction. Its state lives in memory and resets on reload. It does not read or write campaign saves or Supabase data.

## Map-only ownership

`previewModels.ts` and `previewModelDetails.ts` supply original Three.js village and external resource-building models exclusively to `PreviewScene.ts`. The city view does not import this folder. No city components, internal building models, city rules, troop graphics, or battle systems are changed. The city's four internal resource fields remain the responsibility of the separate city implementation; this sample adds no map cells or production for them.

`src/main.tsx` selects the sample only when the query parameter is present. The launcher adds its entry link. The normal game continues to use its existing map and game state.

## Implemented sample rules

- The signed 200 × 200 realm covers canonical coordinates −100 through 99. East joins west and north joins south, including corners. Terrain height, slopes, grain, rivers, lakes and scenery continue across the seams. Most of its area is land, with branching rivers, connected ridges, overlapping lake basins, ancient ruins and scattered woodland and desert patches. Race homelands bias the surroundings without dividing the world into five rigid biome sectors.
- A validated unsigned 32-bit realm seed controls geography, water, scenery and village placement. The default is `1346720329` (`0x50455249`). The same seed recreates the same realm; different seeds change resource patches and neighboring settlements. The scene, inspector and square minimap use that same seed.
- Every generated settlement has eight dry neighboring choices and reachable opportunities for all four resources within six cells, respecting every other protected starting area. This is a minimum viability check; terrain bonuses and the number and distance of richer fields vary. It does not promise every resource's best bonus to every village.
- Five original race compounds occupy at most 0.72 cell width and depth. Resource improvements occupy at most 0.56 cell width and depth. Their broader strategic silhouettes are inspired by Civilization's map readability, with original race architecture and assets. Central village halls change their walls, roofs and facades at population 200, 400, 800 and 1,600.
- Resource buildings improve actual walls, roofs and production equipment at every completed level from 1 through 20. Architectural milestones are field hut (1), work shed (2), timber workshop (3), masonry hall (5), production court (8), powered works (10), advanced complex (15) and master works (20). Farms gain barns, granaries and windmills; lumber mills gain enclosed workshops, saws, waterwheels and loading machinery; mines gain headframes, processing halls and hoists; clay pits gain masonry kilns, chimneys and loading bays. Above level 20, mature architecture is retained and five bounded secondary detail variations are reused. Each race/building pair has at most 24 cached templates, rather than infinitely growing geometry.
- Models include race-specific entrances and courtyards, markets and wells, walls or palisades, golden crops, farm fences, saws and mill wheels, ore carts and mine rails, and clay kilns and brick stacks. Trees clear the larger footprints while scenery outside them stays visible. Detailed silhouettes remain visible further into the regional view, within the existing 64-village detail budget.
- Native scenery uses layered pine and clustered broadleaf crowns, shrubs and scrub, dry oasis-bank palms, broken arches and occasional rune stones. Ten shared geometry families stay instanced by region; small foliage hides beyond view span 70, and forest detail beyond 110. Close-view cached shadows use a 2,048 × 2,048 map. These quality changes need real GPU/browser verification; automated geometry checks do not establish frame rates.
- Willowford starts with no claimed external land and four claim slots. The player chooses four of the eight immediately neighboring cells, including diagonals. All four initial choices must stay within that first ring, even if population has already unlocked more slots.
- Claims start empty at level 0, produce nothing, and remain natural in the scene. Construction is a separate action. Every resource building can be built on any land terrain; water is unavailable.
- Terrain modifiers apply to the normal building rate. Grassland farms receive +10%; mountain farms receive −25%. The inspector shows potential before claiming.
- Completed construction and upgrades add ten people. Population 120 unlocks a fifth external plot; each subsequent 40 people unlock another. Later claims must connect to the village or its claims, stay within six cells of the center, and avoid other villages' protected starting land. Distance, protection, adjacency, resource reachability and claim ownership use the same wrapped topology; coordinate aliases always refer to one canonical field.
- Construction takes six seconds for this sample. Resource rates remain units per hour. Initial supplies allow testing without waiting for internal city production. Completed models appear only when their construction finishes; upgrades change output and geometry.

## Controls

Drag to pan, scroll or pinch to zoom, right-drag or use rotation buttons to rotate. Arrow keys pan, Q/E rotate, H returns home, and 0 shows the world. The minimap supports clicking and dragging; the homeland selector visits each race's capital. Field search, **Zoom to field**, and the neighboring-field chooser focus a closer field view. Canvas selection alone does not move the camera. Clicking visible roofs or flags selects their owning field even where their raised silhouettes project over neighboring terrain. Culled models and models hidden behind foreground terrain cannot steal a field click.

Keep panning past any edge to continue from the opposite side. Field search accepts whole-coordinate aliases (for example, X=100 focuses X=−100). The minimap splits a seam-crossing viewport into matching pieces on opposite edges. **World** shows one canonical realm; exploration repeats terrain/scenery with shared geometry and instance buffers, and uses shared model assets for visible settlement copies. The isolated `PreviewCamera.ts` leaves the campaign camera's original bounds unchanged. Exploration zoom is bounded so a 3 × 3 set of native tiles covers the camera, including rotated views; the overview frames the full realm on portrait and landscape screens.

**Realm seed** opens the seed control. **Randomize** chooses a new seed; **Start realm** resets the sample to that seed with empty player plots. Reusing the current seed restarts the same realm. Invalid values leave the existing sample intact. State is temporary; reloading returns to the default realm.

## Integration and verification limits

This sample is not a multiplayer authority or a database migration. Production use needs server-owned population, costs, queues, claim capacity and ownership; atomic competing-claim validation; persistence; and an agreed city/map data contract. The sample population curve and six-second queues are demonstration balance. Economic levels are currently uncapped; resource geometry uses a finite number of stage/detail templates. Thin map borders reflect actual sample ownership, with amber for the player's land and subdued race colors for other settlements. They do not imply implemented alliances or diplomacy.

Domain and native-geometry tests live in `tests/map-preview-domain.test.ts`, `tests/preview-renderer.test.ts` and `tests/preview-building-evolution.test.ts`. They verify architecture independently of resource piles, periodic heights/slopes, canonical ownership, navigation, seam picking, minimap footprints and shared-asset disposal. Run the repository's normal test and build commands. Browser graphics, touch hardware performance, and large-player load testing require separate verification; see the current root `design-qa.md` record.
